FOMC Vote - 9 for / 3 dissents
Hammack (+25bp)Kashkari (+25bp)Logan (+25bp)

Three members dissented preferring to raise the target range by 25bp. The remaining 9 members voted to maintain the rate unchanged. Warsh's second FOMC meeting as Chair.

Key Quote
For some households, businesses, and market professionals, five years of high inflation have left a mistaken impression that is hard to shake: that the Fed's implicit inflation target was somehow above 2 percent. Let me reiterate: There is no soft inflation target, there is no soft implicit target — not on this Committee's watch. There is only a target, and it is 2 percent.
- Kevin Warsh, Chair of the Federal Reserve
Investment Implications
Hike risk is now on the table - 3 dissents pushing for +25bp
First hawkish dissents since tightening cycle. If inflation stays elevated, next move could be a hike, not a hold.
Warsh closes the door on soft target — 2% is non-negotiable
Explicit rejection of any implicit target above 2%. Signals a longer and more restrictive path than markets may have priced.
Market yields surging — financial conditions tightening without Fed action
Treasury yields moved at top-decile pace inter-meeting. Fed welcomed this. Balance sheet as accommodation vector under review.
AI capex boom — inflationary or structural supply boost?
Nearly 20% growth in AI-related equipment. Warsh flagged uncertainty: could broaden inflation or build future supply. Key open question.
Economy resilient — no recession signal, no labor market deterioration
Solid growth, stable unemployment. Hard landing risk remains low for now despite restrictive rates.

FOMC Vote - 12 for / 0 dissents
WarshWilliamsBarrBowmanCookHammackJeffersonKashkariLoganMiranPaulsonWaller

First unanimous vote since January 2026. Warsh's first FOMC meeting as Chair. No forward guidance in the statement for the first time in years.

Key Quote
We recognize that inflation has been running well ahead of the Fed's long-stated inflation goal of 2 percent — that's been going on for more than 5 years. Persistently high prices are a burden for the American people. But the recent past need not be prologue. Members of the FOMC are unambiguous and unanimous: This Committee will deliver price stability.
- Kevin Warsh, Chair of the Federal Reserve

Framework transition creates communication uncertainty
Without forward guidance, markets must re-anchor to data. Higher noise and volatility in rate expectations.
Energy inflation persistent - Middle East risk premium embedded
CPI 4,2% in May. Supply shock not yet resolved. Duration remains key variable for rate path.
Unanimous vote signals institutional cohesion under new Chair
No dissents for first time since Jan '26. Unified front reduces tail risk of policy surprise at next meeting.
